Overview

What is at stake, and what came back.

Did it work?

Money recovered because of the agent — not merely alongside it.

Gross recovery cannot tell the two apart: some failed charges come back on their own when a customer tops up or an issuer stops declining. A randomised slice of cases is therefore held out — detected and diagnosed like any other, then never intervened on. The gap between the arms is the agent's effect; everything else would have happened anyway.

The loop

What actually happened to the events that arrived.

Every number below is a row count in the database. Two gates sit inside the loop — one before a decision is made, one immediately before it executes — because state can change in between.

What it refuses to do

The part of the system that says no.

Four invariants, enforced in a module that imports neither the policy table nor the model — so a wrong policy cell cannot route around them and no model output can reach them. They are checked before a decision and again before execution. The counts are live: this is how often each one actually stopped or delayed something in this batch.

How it chooses

A lookup, not a judgement call.

The intervention comes from a static table indexed by (failure cause, attempt number). It is total — every cell is filled — so the lookup cannot fall through to a default, and every decision records the exact cell it came from.
